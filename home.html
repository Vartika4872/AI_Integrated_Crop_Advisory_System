    Chemical Pest Control

    ₹189

    Target harmful insects such as aphids, beetles, and caterpillars. Common examples include: Pyrethroids: Synthetic chemicals like permethrin.

    • Control fungal diseases like mildew or blight that can infect crops.
    • Used to control unwanted weeds that compete with crops for nutrients and space.
    • For controlling rodents that damage crops, though they are not used as frequently in crop production.

    Chemical pest control involves the use of synthetic or natural chemicals to manage or eliminate pests, including insects, weeds, fungi, and other organisms that can harm crops. These chemicals are typically applied in the form of sprays, dusts, or granules.

    Biological Pest Control

    ₹290

    Biological pest control is the use of natural organisms—such as predators, parasites, or pathogens—to manage or reduce pest populations in agricultural settings. This approach aims to control pests without relying on synthetic chemicals, making it an environmentally friendly and sustainable option.

    • Natural Predators: These are beneficial organisms that eat or hunt pests.
    • Parasitoids: Organisms that lay eggs on or inside pests, and their larvae feed on the pest, eventually killing it.
    • Pathogens: Disease-causing organisms that specifically target pests.

    Biological pest control is often integrated into broader pest management systems (like Integrated Pest Management or IPM) to ensure crop protection while maintaining ecological balance.

    Mechanical and Physical Controls

    ₹99

    Mechanical and physical pest control methods involve using physical barriers or techniques to prevent, trap, or remove pests without the need for chemicals. These methods are often labor-intensive but can be highly effective in minimizing pest damage while being environmentally friendly.

    • Row Covers: Lightweight fabric placed over crops to prevent insects (like aphids or caterpillars) from reaching plants.
    • Planting crops that are more attractive to pests than the main crops, thereby diverting pests away from valuable plants
    • Manually removing pests from plants, especially larger insects like caterpillars or beetles, to reduce pest populations.

    Mechanical and physical controls are most effective when combined with other pest management methods (like biological or chemical control) as part of an Integrated Pest Management (IPM) approach.
